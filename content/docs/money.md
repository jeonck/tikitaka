---
title: Money & Shopping
weight: 13
---

English speakers talk about *things* freely and about *amounts* carefully. You can discuss a purchase, a shop, a bargain, or whether something was worth it all day long — but naming exact figures, especially your own, is a much smaller door. These answers stay on the comfortable side of that line.

{{< tk q="Where did you get that?" ctx="Someone noticing your bag, jacket, or headphones." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Online —" say="Online, actually. Nowhere exciting." back="Why, are you looking for one?" why="Nowhere exciting preempts the follow-up when the answer is boring. The question redirects to the reason they asked." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="A little place —" say="A little place near the station that I only found because I was hiding from the rain. I have been back three times since." back="Do you ever find things that way?" why="The story of how you found it is more interesting than the shop name, and the accident is what makes it tellable." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Dangerous question —" say="Dangerous question. If I tell you, you are going to end up on the same website I have been avoiding all month." back="How is your self-control these days?" why="Framing a recommendation as a warning is a common, friendly move. It makes them want the answer more, not less." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Was it expensive?" ctx="A friend, direct enough to ask." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not too bad —" say="Not too bad, actually. It was on sale." back="" why="Not too bad is the standard way to answer without a number. On sale explains it and closes the topic cleanly." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="More than I planned —" say="More than I planned, less than I feared. I told myself it was an investment, which is what I say every time." back="Do you talk yourself into things too?" why="A range instead of a figure keeps it comfortable. Admitting the self-justification is far more relatable than the price." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="We do not discuss it —" say="We do not discuss it in this house. It is between me and my bank." back="Have you got one of those purchases?" why="A joke is the standard polite refusal for a price question. It declines without any awkwardness on either side." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Are you looking for anything in particular?" ctx="A shop assistant, thirty seconds after you walk in." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Just browsing —" say="Just browsing for now, thanks." back="" why="Just browsing is the fixed phrase and every assistant expects it. For now softens it so you can still ask for help later." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Actually, yes —" say="Actually, yes. I am after something for a cold office, so warm but not too heavy." back="Do you have anything like that?" why="I am after is a natural alternative to I am looking for. Describing the use case gets you better help than naming a product." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Not yet —" say="Not yet, but give me ten minutes and I am sure I will have talked myself into something." back="What is going fast at the moment?" why="Light and honest about how shopping actually goes. Asking what is popular gives the assistant an easy, low-pressure opening." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Should we split it?" ctx="The bill has just arrived." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Down the middle —" say="Yeah, down the middle is easiest." back="Have you got cash or shall we do it by card?" why="Down the middle is the idiom for an equal split. Naming the payment method next keeps the moment moving." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Let me get this —" say="Let me get this one. You paid last time and I have been meaning to return the favor." back="Next one is on you?" why="Return the favor and next one is on you are the two phrases that make paying feel like a rhythm rather than a debt." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Only if —" say="Only if we agree not to do the thing where we both reach for it and argue for five minutes." back="Deal?" why="Naming the ritual everyone performs is disarming and funny. Deal is a one-word close that almost always gets a yes." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Is it worth it?" ctx="Someone considering the same purchase or subscription you already made." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="For me, yes —" say="For me, yes. I use it most days." back="Would you?" why="For me is the hedge that makes a recommendation safe. Frequency of use is the most convincing evidence there is." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Depends how often —" say="It depends how often you would actually use it. Every day, absolutely. Once a month, I would borrow one first." back="How often do you think you would reach for it?" why="Converting worth into a usage threshold is genuinely helpful advice, and it hands the decision back to them." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I am the wrong person —" say="I am the wrong person to ask. I have already spent the money, so my brain is fully committed to telling you yes." back="What is holding you back?" why="Admitting your own bias is charming and unusually honest. The follow-up gets to the real hesitation." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< callout type="info" >}}
The most useful move on this page is **swapping a number for a range or a reason** — *more than I planned*, *not too bad*, *it was on sale*. You have answered fully, and no figure ever entered the room.
{{< /callout >}}
