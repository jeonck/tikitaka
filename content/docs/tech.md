Everyone has a relationship with their devices and nobody thinks theirs is ideal — which makes this the easiest topic to be interesting on. A confession beats a specification every time. Nobody needs your phone model; they want to know how you actually live with it.

{{< tk q="Are you on any of the socials?" ctx="Someone deciding how to stay in touch with you." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="A couple —" say="A couple, but I am mostly a reader rather than a poster." back="Where are you most?" why="Reader rather than poster is a compact way to describe low-key usage. Where are you most is how people actually ask which app to use." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="I deleted most —" say="I deleted most of them last year and kept one, and honestly I have not missed the others once." back="Have you ever done a clear-out like that?" why="A deliberate decision is more interesting than a list of apps, and clear-out is the natural word for it." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Technically yes —" say="Technically yes, in the sense that the accounts exist and I visit them roughly twice a year like distant relatives." back="Are you an every-day person?" why="Technically yes is a very natural way to admit something is true but not meaningful. The simile does the rest." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Do you use AI for that?" ctx="A colleague seeing how fast you turned something around." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="For parts of it —" say="For parts of it, yeah. Mostly the first draft." back="Do you?" why="For parts of it is precise and modest. First draft is the clearest way to describe where most people actually use it." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="For the boring half —" say="For the boring half. It gets me to something I can react to, and reacting is much faster for me than starting from nothing." back="Where does it actually help you?" why="Naming the mechanism — something to react to — is a real insight rather than a yes. It invites a comparison of methods." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I did, and then —" say="I did, and then I spent longer checking it than I would have spent writing it. So the answer is yes, with regrets." back="Have you had one of those go badly?" why="With regrets is a dry, very English construction. Inviting a failure story is more fun than swapping recommendations." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Can you send me that?" ctx="Mid-conversation, someone wants the link, file, or photo." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Sending it now —" say="Yeah, sending it now." back="Let me know if it does not come through." why="Present continuous makes it feel immediate. Come through is the standard phrase for a message arriving." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Give me a second —" say="Give me a second to find it. It is buried in a chat from about March, so this may take some scrolling." back="Do you want the whole thread or just the link?" why="Narrating the delay stops it being awkward, and the closing question saves a second round of messages." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Remind me —" say="Remind me tonight, because if you leave it to my memory it is going to arrive some time next year." back="Are you a straight-away person or a remind-me person?" why="Honest about your own follow-through, which people find endearing rather than unreliable, and it sets a realistic expectation." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Sorry, you cut out — can you say that again?" ctx="A video call with a bad connection." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="No problem —" say="No problem. I was saying the deadline moved to Thursday." back="Did that come through?" why="Repeating the content immediately, without re-explaining the whole point, is what a fluent speaker does. Did that come through checks without fuss." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Let me start again —" say="Let me start again from the top, and shout if it goes again. The deadline moved to Thursday, so the review has to happen tomorrow." back="Are you hearing me okay now?" why="From the top and shout if it goes are both natural call phrases. Restating in full is safer than guessing what they missed." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="That was probably me —" say="That was probably my end. My connection has been dropping all morning and I have started blaming the weather." back="Can you hear me now, or shall I dial back in?" why="Taking the blame for a connection problem is the frictionless move. Dial back in is the phrase for rejoining a call." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="How do you keep up with all this stuff?" ctx="Someone who thinks you are more on top of technology than you are." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="I really do not —" say="I really do not. I just hear about things late and pretend otherwise." back="Do you follow any of it?" why="Denying the premise is honest and instantly likeable. Nobody actually keeps up, and saying so is a relief." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="One newsletter —" say="One newsletter on a Sunday, and that is the whole system. Anything that does not survive a week of me ignoring it was probably not important." back="How do you filter it?" why="Describing a deliberately small system is more useful than a list of sources, and filter is the right verb for the shared problem." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I have given up —" say="I have completely given up and I recommend it. Whatever matters will come and find me eventually." back="Are you still trying, or have you made peace with it?" why="Made peace with it is a lovely phrase for accepting something you cannot control, and it invites a genuine opinion." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< callout type="info" >}}
Technology questions are a trap for over-detail. If your answer contains a model number, a version, or a spec, cut it — replace it with what the thing does *to your day*. That is the part anyone can answer back on.
{{< /callout >}}
