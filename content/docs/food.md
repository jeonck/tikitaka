---
title: Food
weight: 5
---

Food is the easiest topic in any language, because everyone has an opinion and nobody is wrong. It is also where a lot of learners default to *it is delicious* and stop.

Try to say *what kind* of good. Specific beats enthusiastic.

{{< tk q="Have you eaten yet?" ctx="Late morning or early evening — often an offer in disguise." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not yet —" say="Not yet, actually. I was just starting to think about it." back="Were you about to go somewhere?" why="Not yet plus were you about to go somewhere turns a question into an invitation without you having to ask directly." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="I grabbed something —" say="I grabbed something at my desk, which barely counts. It was a sandwich I do not remember eating." back="Have you had a proper one?" why="Barely counts is a nice hedge, and the forgettable sandwich is a small joke that keeps it light." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Depends what counts —" say="Depends what counts as eating. If coffee counts then yes, I have had three meals." back="Are you going somewhere good?" why="Playing with the definition is a natural move and lands well when someone is clearly about to suggest lunch." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="What are you in the mood for?" ctx="Choosing a place with a friend or colleague." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="I am easy —" say="I am easy, honestly. Anything except what we had yesterday." back="What are you leaning towards?" why="I am easy is the standard phrase for no strong preference. Adding one exclusion is still helpful and prevents an endless loop." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Something warm —" say="Something warm and not too heavy. I have got an afternoon of meetings and I do not want to fall asleep in them." back="Does that rule out the noodle place for you?" why="Giving a constraint and the reason behind it makes deciding much faster than naming a cuisine." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I will decide —" say="I will decide the moment you suggest something I do not want. That is the only way I know what I want." back="So go on, suggest something terrible." why="A genuinely funny and very human observation. It also efficiently gets the other person to commit first." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="How is everything?" cue="they check" ctx="A server, mid-meal. The answer is almost always short." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Really good, thanks —" say="Really good, thank you." back="" why="This is a courtesy check, not a conversation. A short warm answer is the correct one and needs no pass back." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Better than I expected —" say="Really good — the sauce especially. I was not expecting it to be this rich." back="Is that the one everyone orders?" why="One specific compliment is worth more than a general one, and staff usually enjoy answering what is popular." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Could we get —" say="All good, thank you. Could we get a bit more water when you have a moment?" back="" why="If something is genuinely missing, this is the polite shape — praise first, request second, when you have a moment as the softener." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Do you cook much?" ctx="Anywhere. A reliable second-round question." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not as much as —" say="Not as much as I should. I have got about four meals I can make well and I rotate them forever." back="Are you a cook?" why="Four meals on rotation is a very recognisable situation and much better than a plain no." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="More than I used to —" say="More than I used to. I started properly during a stretch when I was broke, and it turned out I actually enjoy it." back="Did you learn from someone, or just work it out?" why="A reason for starting gives the answer a shape, and the follow-up opens a family or self-taught story." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I assemble —" say="I would not call it cooking. I assemble. There is a difference and my flatmate points it out regularly." back="Are you an actual cook, or an assembler like me?" why="Inventing a distinction — cooking versus assembling — is playful and gives them a category to claim." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Can you handle spicy food?" ctx="Ordering together, or someone testing your limits." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Up to a point —" say="Up to a point. I can enjoy it, but I am not trying to prove anything." back="Are you the one who orders it extra hot?" why="Up to a point is an honest middle answer that avoids both bragging and refusing." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="I have been training —" say="I have been training, genuinely. Two years ago I could not do it at all and now I am somewhere in the middle." back="Did you grow up eating spicy food?" why="Framing tolerance as something you built is more interesting than a fixed yes or no, and it invites their background." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I say yes —" say="I always say yes and then regret it about four bites in. Every time." back="Are you going to order something that ruins me?" why="Predicting your own bad decision is funny and warm, and it playfully hands them control of the order." >}}
{{< /tk >}}
