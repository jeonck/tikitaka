---
title: Screens & Sound
weight: 15
---

Films, shows, and music are the safest opinions in English — strong feelings are welcome, and disagreeing is part of the fun. The only real failure here is the plot summary. Nobody wants the story; they want to know what it *did* to you.

{{< tk q="Seen anything good lately?" ctx="A friend or coworker looking for a recommendation." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not really —" say="Not really. I keep starting things and not finishing them." back="Have you got anything?" why="Admitting you have nothing is fine and extremely common. Redirecting to them is the whole point of the question anyway." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="One thing —" say="One thing, actually. I watched a documentary about deep-sea divers and I have thought about it roughly once a day since." back="Are you a documentary person?" why="The measure of a recommendation is its aftereffect, not its plot. Once a day since is a far stronger endorsement than good." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Define good —" say="Define good. I have watched eleven hours of a show I would not defend in public." back="What is your guilty one?" why="A guilty pleasure is one of the easiest things to trade. Would not defend in public is a funny, natural construction." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="What kind of music are you into?" ctx="A car journey, a party, or someone about to hand you the aux cable." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Bit of everything —" say="A bit of everything, honestly. Depends on the day." back="What about you?" why="A bit of everything is the standard non-answer. It is slightly boring, which is exactly why it is safe with a stranger." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Depends what I am doing —" say="It depends what I am doing. Something with no words if I am working, and something loud and slightly embarrassing if I am cooking." back="Do you have a cooking playlist?" why="Sorting music by activity is more revealing and more relatable than naming genres. The follow-up is specific and fun." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I have not updated —" say="I have not updated my taste since about nineteen, and at this point I have decided that is a personality rather than a problem." back="Are you still discovering things?" why="Owning a mild flaw is charming. It also quietly gives them permission to admit the same thing." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Have you watched it yet?" ctx="A show everyone around you is talking about." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not yet —" say="Not yet, no. It is on the list." back="No spoilers." why="On the list is the universal holding phrase. No spoilers is the fixed request and it is never taken badly." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Two episodes in —" say="Two episodes in. I am enjoying it, but I have been warned that it changes completely around episode four." back="Where are you up to?" why="Where are you up to is the exact phrase for comparing progress, and it prevents either of you spoiling the other." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I am the last —" say="I am apparently the last person alive who has not, and at this point I am holding out on principle." back="Is it actually as good as everyone says?" why="Being deliberately behind is a real and funny position. Asking whether the hype is deserved always gets a strong answer." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Did you like it?" ctx="Walking out of a cinema, or the credits just rolled." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Yeah, I did —" say="Yeah, I did. Better than I expected." back="Did you?" why="Better than I expected is a low-risk positive. It leaves you room to adjust once you hear their opinion." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Mostly —" say="Mostly. The first hour was excellent and then I think it lost its nerve at the end." back="Did the ending work for you?" why="Praise then a specific reservation is how real opinions sound. Lost its nerve is a vivid, natural criticism." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Ask me tomorrow —" say="Ask me tomorrow. Right now I am somewhere between impressed and annoyed and I cannot tell which is winning." back="What did you make of it?" why="Delaying a verdict is honest after something complicated. What did you make of it invites a fuller answer than did you like it." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="What are you listening to?" ctx="Someone notices your headphones and asks." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Just a podcast —" say="Just a podcast, nothing special." back="" why="Nothing special politely closes a question that was mostly a passing curiosity. Not every question wants a full answer." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="An old album —" say="An old album I have listened to about four hundred times. It is basically background noise for me now, but I cannot work without it." back="Do you have one of those?" why="A comfort album is a near-universal experience. Background noise you cannot work without is a nice, specific contradiction." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Nothing —" say="Nothing at all, actually. The headphones are just so nobody talks to me." back="Is that a terrible thing to admit?" why="A small confession delivered lightly. Asking whether it is terrible is a warm way to check they took it as a joke." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Shall I put something on?" ctx="An evening at someone's place, the room has gone quiet." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Go for it —" say="Go for it. I am easy." back="Your house, your choice." why="I am easy is the compact English phrase for having no preference. Deferring to the host closes it warmly." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Something we can talk over —" say="Something we can talk over, ideally. If it is too good I will stop listening to you entirely and then feel bad about it." back="Have you got a go-to for this?" why="Naming what the music is for helps them choose well. Go-to is the natural word for a reliable default." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I will allow it —" say="I will allow it, but I reserve the right to make one face if it goes badly wrong." back="How confident are you feeling?" why="A mock veto is a friendly way to stay involved in a choice you have just handed over." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Have you read anything good lately?" ctx="Someone who has spotted a book, or is looking for one." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not lately —" say="Not lately, honestly. I have three started and none finished." back="Have you got one on the go?" why="On the go is the standard phrase for a book in progress, and three started none finished is a very common state." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="One I keep recommending —" say="One I keep recommending badly, because every time I try to describe it I make it sound worse than it is. It is about a family and almost nothing happens." back="Do you mind a slow one?" why="Admitting you cannot summarize it is intriguing rather than off-putting, and the question checks their taste first." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I have started reading —" say="I have started reading the same forty pages of the same book for about six months. At this point we are just companions." back="Are you a finisher?" why="The permanently unfinished book is a very warm thing to admit. Finisher is a nice, simple category to be asked about." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Do you play games at all?" ctx="Someone testing whether this is common ground." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not really —" say="Not really. I know I am missing something." back="What are you playing?" why="Conceding you are missing out is friendlier than a flat no, and it hands the topic straight to the person who cares." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="One at a time —" say="One at a time, and slowly. I am about forty hours into something everyone else finished two years ago, and I am in no hurry at all." back="Are you a finish-it or a wander-around player?" why="Describing how you play is more interesting than what you play, and it works even if they know none of your titles." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Only on trains —" say="Only on trains and in waiting rooms, which means my entire experience of gaming is about eleven minutes at a time." back="Does that count?" why="Defining your relationship by context rather than genre is funny and specific, and does that count invites a friendly ruling." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Is it worth sticking with?" ctx="They are three episodes in and losing patience." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Give it one more —" say="Give it one more. It turns a corner around episode five." back="If it does not click by then, drop it." why="Naming the exact episode makes the advice actionable. Giving permission to quit makes the recommendation trustworthy." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Depends what you want —" say="It depends what you want from it. If you are waiting for the plot to speed up, no. If you are happy watching people be strange at each other, absolutely." back="Which one are you in the mood for?" why="Conditional recommendations are far more useful than a verdict, and the closing question decides it for them." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I am not objective —" say="I am not objective about this one, so ignore me entirely. I would defend it against any evidence you presented." back="What is annoying you about it?" why="Admitting bias makes your enthusiasm charming rather than pushy, and it gets to their actual complaint." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Do you mind spoilers?" ctx="Before someone tells you about something you have not seen." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="I would rather not —" say="I would rather not, if that is okay." back="Tell me once I have seen it." why="A polite refusal plus a promise to come back keeps their enthusiasm alive instead of shutting it down." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Small ones are fine —" say="Small ones are fine. I do not mind knowing what kind of thing happens, I just do not want to know when." back="Can you tell it that way?" why="Defining what counts as a spoiler for you is genuinely helpful, and most people have never thought about the distinction." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I actively want them —" say="I actively want them. I read the ending first and I have never once regretted it, and I am aware this makes me a monster." back="How badly does that upset you?" why="An unpopular opinion stated confidently is fun. It usually produces a strong reaction and a much better conversation." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< callout type="info" >}}
The strongest **Add** on this page is never a description — it is an *effect*. Thought about it once a day, cannot work without it, lost its nerve at the end. Give someone the effect and they will supply their own.
{{< /callout >}}
