title: Work

The trap with work questions is the job title. *I am a backend developer* is accurate and completely unanswerable — unless the other person happens to be one too.

Say what you do in terms of the problem, not the title. Problems are something anyone can ask about.

{{< tk q="So, what do you do?" ctx="A party, a meetup, someone you just met." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="I work in —" say="I work in software, on the infrastructure side. Basically I keep things running that nobody notices until they break." back="What about you?" why="Job title plus a plain-language translation. The second sentence is the part they can respond to." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="The short version —" say="The short version is I am an engineer. The longer version is that I spend most of my week making other engineers wait less." back="Does your work have a short version and a long version too?" why="Offering a short and long version is a very natural English move. It lets them choose how deep to go." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Depends who is asking —" say="Depends who is asking. At family dinners I say I work with computers and everyone nods and changes the subject." back="Do you get that too, or does your job actually explain itself?" why="Turns a dull question into a shared complaint. Almost everybody has a job their relatives do not understand." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="How's work these days?" ctx="A friend catching up." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Busy but fine —" say="Busy but fine. Nothing on fire, which I will take." back="How about yours?" why="Nothing on fire is common workplace shorthand for no crisis. It signals mild relief without complaining." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Up and down —" say="Up and down. The project itself is interesting, but we are short two people, so everything takes twice as long as it should." back="Are you fully staffed on your side?" why="One good thing and one hard thing is the most honest shape for this answer, and understaffing is something almost everyone can match." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Ask me on a Friday —" say="Ask me on a Friday and I will say great. Ask me on a Monday and you will get a very different answer." back="Which day are you asking on?" why="A joke that is also true. It answers the question sideways while sounding relaxed rather than evasive." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="What's your team like?" ctx="An interview, or a new colleague sizing up the place." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Pretty small —" say="Pretty small, about six of us, and everyone is easy to work with." back="How big is yours?" why="Size plus one adjective. Enough to answer, short enough to hand back immediately." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Mixed bag —" say="Mixed bag, in a good way. Half the team has been here for years and half of us joined last spring, so there is a lot of asking questions out loud." back="Is yours more settled, or still forming?" why="A concrete detail about how the team behaves beats an adjective. Asking questions out loud is a picture they can see." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Loud —" say="Loud. In a good way — we argue about things in the open, which took me a while to get used to." back="Is your team the quiet type or the loud type?" why="One surprising word first, then the explanation. It makes people lean in and then gives them an easy binary to answer." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Busy week?" ctx="Passing in the corridor, or the start of a call." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Yeah, pretty full —" say="Yeah, pretty full. Nothing terrible, just a lot of small things." back="You?" why="Pretty full is softer than crazy or slammed and does not invite sympathy you do not need." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Front-loaded —" say="Front-loaded, thankfully. Monday and Tuesday were brutal, but it should quiet down by Thursday." back="Is yours back-loaded or are you clear?" why="Front-loaded and back-loaded are natural in work English and make the answer more precise than busy." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Define busy —" say="Define busy. If busy means productive then no, but if it means back-to-back meetings then absolutely." back="Which kind is your week?" why="A gentle push back on the word itself. It sounds witty and gives them a much more interesting question to answer." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="How did you get into that?" ctx="They are genuinely curious about your path." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Kind of by accident —" say="Kind of by accident. I started in something else, took one project that touched this, and never left." back="Was yours planned or accidental?" why="Most career stories are accidental, so this is both true and instantly relatable." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="It started with —" say="It started with a broken laptop, honestly. I spent a whole weekend fixing it myself instead of paying someone, and I liked the weekend more than I expected." back="Was there a moment like that for you?" why="A single concrete origin scene is far stickier than a summary of your CV. People remember the laptop." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="The honest answer —" say="The honest answer is that I was bad at the thing I studied and good at the thing I did for fun, and eventually I stopped fighting it." back="Did you end up doing what you studied?" why="Self-aware honesty invites the same, and very few people ended up doing exactly what they studied." >}}
{{< /tk >}}
