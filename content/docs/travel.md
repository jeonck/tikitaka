---
title: Travel & Places
weight: 6
---

Place questions carry more weight than they look like they do. *Where are you from* can be small talk, or it can be the start of someone genuinely wanting to know you.

Give them a version they can follow up on, and keep control of how much you share.

{{< tk q="Where are you from originally?" ctx="Someone has noticed you are not local." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Originally from —" say="Originally from Seoul, but I have been here long enough that I say we about this city now." back="Are you from around here?" why="One place plus one sign of belonging. It answers the question without turning you into a permanent visitor." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="A bit complicated —" say="A bit complicated. Born in one city, grew up in another, and I have lived here longer than either of them." back="Are you from here originally, or did you land here too?" why="A bit complicated is a friendly way to signal there is a longer story without telling all of it yet." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Depends what you mean —" say="Depends what you mean by from. My passport says one thing and my accent says something else entirely." back="Where is home for you?" why="Gently reframes the question and replaces it with a better one. Home is a warmer word than from." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="How do you like it here?" ctx="A local, genuinely wanting to know." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Really settled in —" say="I have really settled in. It took a while, but it feels like home now." back="Have you always lived here?" why="Settled in is the natural verb for this, and admitting it took a while makes it sound honest rather than polite." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="The thing that surprised me —" say="The thing that surprised me is how much I use the parks. I never thought of myself as an outdoors person until I lived somewhere with good ones." back="What is the part people usually miss about this place?" why="A specific surprise is far better than good. The follow-up flatters their local knowledge and always gets an answer." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Ask me in winter —" say="Ask me in winter and you will get a different answer. Right now, though, I have no complaints." back="Is winter as bad as everyone warns me?" why="A seasonal joke that locals almost always want to weigh in on. It reliably produces a long, friendly reply." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Have you traveled much?" ctx="Getting to know someone." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not as much as —" say="Not as much as I would like. A few places, mostly nearby, nothing dramatic." back="Are you a big traveller?" why="A modest answer that does not apologise. The pass back moves it to the person more likely to have stories." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Fewer places, longer stays —" say="Fewer places but longer stays, if that makes sense. I would rather spend two weeks in one city than see five in a fortnight." back="Which type are you?" why="Stating a travel philosophy instead of a list turns a résumé question into an opinion question." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Enough to know —" say="Enough to know I am terrible at it. I overpack, I get lost, and I still book flights that leave at six in the morning." back="Are you an organised traveller, or is it chaos?" why="Three quick concrete failures land better than one general statement, and everyone has a packing opinion." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Any trips coming up?" ctx="Late in a conversation, or around a holiday." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Nothing booked —" say="Nothing booked, but I have been looking. That is usually the stage I stay in for months." back="Have you got anything planned?" why="Honest about the gap between browsing and booking, which is where most people actually live." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="One thing, finally —" say="One thing, finally. A few days by the coast next month — no plan beyond walking and eating, which is the whole point." back="Do you plan yours out, or wing it?" why="Naming what the trip is for is more evocative than naming the destination, and the follow-up is a real personality question." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="In my head —" say="Constantly, in my head. I have about six trips fully planned that I will never take." back="What is the one you keep almost booking?" why="The almost-booked trip is something nearly everyone has, and asking for it usually unlocks real enthusiasm." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="What's the best place you've been?" ctx="A question that can freeze you if you try to be accurate." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Hard to pick, but —" say="Hard to pick, but probably a small town on the coast I ended up in by accident. Nothing to do there, which was the appeal." back="What is yours?" why="Do not agonise over best. Pick one, give a reason, pass it back — the reason is the interesting part anyway." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Not the most impressive —" say="Not the most impressive place, but the one I think about most is a night market I stumbled into. I did not understand a word and had the best meal of that year." back="Have you had one of those accidental ones?" why="Choosing memorable over impressive gives you a story rather than a landmark, and accidental discoveries are a topic everyone can match." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Wrong question —" say="Best is too hard. Ask me the one I would go back to tomorrow and I can answer instantly." back="What is your go-back-tomorrow place?" why="Swapping the question for an easier one is a legitimate conversational move, and the replacement is genuinely more fun to answer." >}}
{{< /tk >}}
