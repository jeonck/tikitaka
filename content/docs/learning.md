These are the questions you get *because* you are a learner, and they arrive more often than any of the others. The instinct is to apologize. Do not — an apology hands the other person a job. Answer like someone doing something difficult on purpose, because you are.

{{< tk q="How long have you been learning English?" ctx="Someone genuinely curious, not testing you." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Years, on and off —" say="Years, on and off. Properly for about two." back="Do you speak anything else?" why="Separating on and off from properly is honest and precise. Turning it into a question about their languages levels the ground." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Since school —" say="Since school, but school English and talking to actual people turned out to be two completely different skills." back="Did you learn a language that way?" why="A distinction most learners feel and few say out loud. It makes you sound thoughtful rather than apologetic." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Long enough to know —" say="Long enough to know how much is left, which is the discouraging part of getting better at anything." back="Have you hit that with something?" why="A true observation about learning in general. It moves the conversation off your English and onto a shared experience." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Your English is really good!" ctx="Well meant, slightly awkward, extremely common." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Thank you —" say="Thank you, that is kind of you." back="" why="Take the compliment and stop. Deflecting it invites them to insist, and then you are stuck in a loop neither of you wanted." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Thanks —" say="Thanks. It comes and goes, honestly. Some days I can say anything and some days I lose the word for spoon." back="Do you speak a second language?" why="Comes and goes is exactly right and very relatable. A small concrete failure is funnier and more human than a general disclaimer." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="You caught me —" say="You caught me on a good day. Come back after six in the evening and it is a different story entirely." back="Are you better in the morning or at night?" why="Redirecting a compliment into a joke about energy is graceful, and the closing question applies to everyone regardless of language." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Sorry, what does that mean?" ctx="They used a word or an idiom and you did not catch it." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Sorry, one second —" say="Sorry, one second. What does that mean?" back="I have not come across it." why="Asking directly is completely normal and costs you nothing. Not come across it is the neutral phrase, better than I do not know." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="I know the words —" say="I know the words individually, but I have never heard them in that order before." back="Is it something people say a lot?" why="A funny and accurate description of hitting an idiom. Asking about frequency tells you whether it is worth remembering." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I was going to nod —" say="I was going to nod and look it up later, but I have decided to be brave about it instead." back="Go on, what does it mean?" why="Admitting the nod-and-look-it-up habit is charming because everyone does it, in every language." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="How do you say that in your language?" ctx="Someone curious after you mentioned a word does not translate." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="We have one word —" say="We have one word for it, but it does not really translate." back="English does not have it either, does it?" why="Does not really translate is the honest and interesting answer. The tag question is a very natural way to invite agreement." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="There is a word —" say="There is a word for it, and it means something closer to the feeling than the thing. Which is why I always end up using three words in English instead." back="Does English have one of those for you?" why="Explaining the shape of the gap is far more interesting than the word itself, and it flatters their curiosity." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I could tell you —" say="I could tell you, but you would have to hear me say it four times and you would still get it wrong." back="Do you want to try?" why="A playful challenge. Offering to teach turns a question about you into an activity you do together." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Are you taking classes, or just picking it up?" ctx="Someone asking how you are learning." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Picking it up —" say="Mostly picking it up. A bit of both, really." back="Have you ever studied one?" why="Picking it up is the standard phrase for informal learning. A bit of both is a comfortable way to avoid over-explaining." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="I stopped classes —" say="I stopped classes and started just talking to people, which is more frightening and about four times faster." back="What worked for you when you learned something new?" why="A clear before-and-after with a reason. Frightening but faster is an opinion people enjoy responding to." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I have a system —" say="I have a system, and the system is watching too much television and calling it study." back="Does that count, do you think?" why="Self-deprecating about a method that genuinely works. Does that count is a light, open question with no wrong answer." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
One habit worth dropping: *Sorry, my English is not good.* It is almost never true by the time you are saying it, and it asks the other person to reassure you instead of continuing the conversation. If you need a moment, say **Let me put that another way** — same pause, none of the apology.
{{< /callout >}}
