---
title: Feelings & Opinions
weight: 7
---

This is the hardest set, because the risk is real. Say too little and you seem closed. Say too much and you have handed someone a weight they did not ask to carry.

The reliable shape is: name the feeling, give it a size, then move the ball. *A bit*, *pretty*, *honestly quite* — the size word is what keeps you in control.

{{< tk q="You okay? You look tired." cue="they notice" ctx="Someone who knows you well enough to say it." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Just a late one —" say="Just a late one, nothing dramatic. I will be fine after coffee." back="Thanks for asking, though." why="Nothing dramatic closes the concern politely. Thanking them keeps the warmth without opening the subject." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="A bit run down —" say="A bit run down, honestly. Nothing wrong, just a stretch of bad sleep and it is catching up with me." back="Are you sleeping alright these days?" why="A bit is the size word doing the work. It is honest without asking them to worry, and sleep is a safe shared topic." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="That obvious? —" say="Is it that obvious? I was hoping I was hiding it better than that." back="Do I look as bad as I feel?" why="Acknowledging you were seen is warm and slightly funny, and it lets you decide afterwards how much to say." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="What did you think of it?" ctx="After a film, a meeting, a presentation." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="I liked it —" say="I liked it. Not sure I would watch it again, but I am glad I saw it." back="What did you make of it?" why="A clean opinion with one honest limit. What did you make of it is a slightly more inviting question than did you like it." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Mixed, actually —" say="Mixed, actually. The first half really had me, and then I felt like it stopped trusting the audience." back="Did it lose you at the same point?" why="Locating exactly where your opinion turned makes it discussable. Asking if it lost them there too invites disagreement safely." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="You first —" say="You first. I want to know if I am the only one who felt that way before I commit to an opinion." back="Go on, be honest." why="A legitimate move when you suspect your view is unpopular, and it usually produces a much franker conversation." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Are you nervous about tomorrow?" ctx="Before something that matters to you." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="A little —" say="A little, but the good kind. I would be more worried if I did not care at all." back="Have you got anything big coming up?" why="The good kind of nervous is a useful phrase — it admits the feeling without asking to be reassured." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="More than I expected —" say="More than I expected, honestly. I was fine all week and then it hit me this morning while I was making breakfast." back="Does it work like that for you too, all at once?" why="A specific moment makes the feeling concrete, and asking how nerves work for them keeps it a conversation rather than a confession." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Ask me at 3am —" say="Not right now. Ask me at three in the morning and you will get a completely different answer." back="Are you the type who sleeps fine before big things?" why="Deflects the intensity with humour while still admitting the truth, and offers an easy question in return." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="How do you feel about the change?" ctx="A reorg, a new manager, a new policy. Careful — this is often not a neutral question." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Too early to say —" say="Too early to say, honestly. I want to see how it works in practice first." back="What is your read on it?" why="A genuinely safe answer when you do not know who you are talking to. Your read is a natural way to ask without committing." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Cautiously positive —" say="Cautiously positive. The reasoning makes sense to me, but I think it is going to be messier in the first few months than anyone is saying." back="Are you seeing the same thing on your side?" why="Cautiously positive lets you support something and flag a concern in one breath — a very useful workplace register." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Honestly, mixed —" say="Honestly, mixed, and I would rather hear where you are on it before I go into mine." back="What has it looked like from where you sit?" why="Signals you have a real opinion while making sure you are not the first to say something risky." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="That must have been rough." cue="they say" ctx="Someone responding to something difficult you shared." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="It was, yeah —" say="It was, yeah. But it is behind me now." back="Thanks for saying that." why="Accepting sympathy directly is a skill. Behind me now signals you do not need the topic to continue." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="At the time —" say="At the time, definitely. Looking back, it is the part I learned the most from, though I would not want to repeat it." back="Have you been through anything similar?" why="Separating how it felt then from how it reads now is emotionally honest, and invites them to share without pressure." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I appreciate that —" say="I appreciate you saying that. Honestly, most people just change the subject." back="Have you had one of those stretches?" why="Naming the kindness makes the moment land, and the pass back turns sympathy into a genuine exchange." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
Route **Flip** is the wrong choice when someone is genuinely worried about you. Humour reads as deflection there. Use **Return** or **Add**, and let the conversation slow down.
{{< /callout >}}
