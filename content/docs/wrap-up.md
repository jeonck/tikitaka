---
title: Wrapping Up
weight: 9
---

Endings are where a good conversation can still go wrong. Leaving too abruptly reads as boredom, and hovering because you cannot find the exit line is worse.

English closings almost always come in two parts: a reason to go, then a warm note. Skip the second part and it sounds like an escape.

{{< tk q="Are you heading out?" ctx="Someone notices you gathering your things." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Yeah, I should —" say="Yeah, I should get going. Long day tomorrow." back="See you Monday?" why="Should get going is the standard phrase. A reason plus a forward-looking question is the complete two-part exit." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="In a minute —" say="In a minute, yeah. I want to say goodbye to a couple of people first, otherwise I will feel rude about it all week." back="Are you off too, or staying?" why="Not yet but soon is a real answer, and it gives them the chance to leave at the same time as you." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I keep saying —" say="I keep saying I am leaving and then finding one more person to talk to. Give me ten minutes." back="Are you sticking around?" why="Self-aware and warm. It signals you are enjoying yourself, which softens the departure entirely." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="It was great meeting you." cue="they say" ctx="End of a first conversation." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="You too —" say="You too, genuinely. I am glad we ended up talking." back="Enjoy the rest of your evening." why="You too is correct and sufficient. Genuinely stops it sounding automatic, which is the whole risk with this exchange." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Likewise — and —" say="Likewise. And I am going to look up that thing you mentioned, so thank you for that." back="Are you usually at these?" why="Referring back to something specific from the conversation is the strongest possible closing move. It proves you were listening." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="You made this —" say="You too. You made this a lot less painful than these things usually are." back="Same time next year?" why="A shared joke about the awkwardness of networking builds instant complicity, and the light question invites a real one." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="We should do this again." cue="they say" ctx="After something that went well." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Definitely —" say="Definitely. I enjoyed that a lot more than I expected to." back="Let me know when you are next free." why="Enthusiastic and honest. Without the follow-up line this is just politeness, so add the invitation to make it real." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Let us actually —" say="Let us actually do it this time, not the version where we say it and then nothing happens for a year." back="Shall I message you next week?" why="Naming the pattern is funny because it is true, and offering to be the one who messages makes it likely to happen." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Hold me to that —" say="Hold me to that. I am terrible at follow-through and I need someone to chase me." back="Will you chase me?" why="Honest about your own weakness and gives them explicit permission to push, which removes the awkwardness of chasing." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Let's keep in touch." cue="they say" ctx="Parting with someone you may not see soon." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="I would like that —" say="I would like that. What is the easiest way to reach you?" back="" why="Turning the sentiment into a concrete exchange of details is the only way keep in touch survives past the doorway." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Please — and —" say="Please do. I am terrible at messaging first, so do not take a slow reply personally." back="Are you on the usual places?" why="Setting expectations about your reply speed now prevents a misread later, and it is disarmingly honest." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="We say that —" say="We both say that every time. Let us actually put something in the calendar before I leave." back="Have you got your phone on you?" why="Refusing the empty version of the phrase, warmly. Very effective if you genuinely want to see them again." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Sorry, I have to take this." cue="they say" ctx="Their phone rings mid-conversation." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Of course —" say="Of course, go ahead." back="" why="Two words, zero friction. Anything longer makes them feel worse about interrupting." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="No problem at all —" say="No problem at all. I will be right here — take your time." back="" why="Right here and take your time both reduce their pressure to rush, which is the kind thing to do." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Perfect timing —" say="Perfect timing, I needed to check my messages anyway. Go on." back="" why="Pretending you also needed the break removes their guilt entirely. A small kindness that sounds like a joke." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< callout type="info" >}}
Almost every card on this page has a **Return** route with no pass back. That is the point of an ending — the last few exchanges should get shorter, not longer. Knowing when to let the ball stop is part of tiki-taka too.
{{< /callout >}}
