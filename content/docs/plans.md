---
title: Making Plans
weight: 8
---

*We should get coffee sometime* is not a plan. It is a signal, and English speakers use it constantly to test whether you are interested before anyone risks a real invitation.

If you want the plan to happen, your answer has to add something concrete — a day, a place, a next step. If you do not, warmth without specifics is the polite decline.

{{< tk q="Want to grab a coffee sometime?" ctx="A colleague or new acquaintance testing the water." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Yeah, I would like that —" say="Yeah, I would like that. Let me know when suits you." back="Are you usually free in the mornings or after work?" why="Warm yes plus a narrowing question. Sometime stays vague forever unless somebody adds a shape." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Definitely — how about —" say="Definitely. How about next week? I am fairly open Tuesday and Thursday." back="Does either of those work for you?" why="The single most effective move in English planning: propose two specific options. It converts a signal into a calendar entry." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Only if you know —" say="Only if you know somewhere good. I have been going to the same place for two years out of pure laziness." back="Have you got a spot?" why="Accepts enthusiastically while handing them an easy job. People enjoy being the one who knows a place." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Are you free Friday?" ctx="Somebody has something in mind but has not said what yet." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Should be —" say="Should be, yeah. Nothing in the calendar so far." back="What were you thinking?" why="Do not commit before you know what it is. What were you thinking is the standard, entirely polite way to ask." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Free after six —" say="Free after six. I have got something in the afternoon that might run over, but the evening is clear." back="Were you thinking early or late?" why="Giving your real constraint upfront saves a whole round of messages and sounds organised rather than reluctant." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="That depends entirely —" say="That depends entirely on what you are about to ask me." back="Go on — what is it?" why="Playful and buys information before commitment. Works with people you already have an easy rapport with." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="What time works for you?" ctx="The plan is agreed, now you are settling details." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Whatever suits —" say="Whatever suits you, I am flexible." back="What is easiest on your end?" why="Fine occasionally, but be careful — two flexible people can bounce this back and forth for days. Use it when you genuinely do not mind." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Anytime after —" say="Anytime after two works. Earlier than that gets tight for me, but I can make it work if it is your only window." back="Does mid-afternoon suit you?" why="A constraint, a concession, and a concrete suggestion. This is the shape that actually ends a scheduling conversation." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I will make it work —" say="Pick a time and I will make it work. I would rather you choose than have us do the polite dance for three messages." back="Just tell me when." why="Naming the polite dance and refusing to do it is friendly, efficient, and very natural among people who know each other." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Do you want to join us?" ctx="A group heading somewhere. You may or may not want to go." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="I would love to, but —" say="I would love to, but I have got to finish something first. Next time for sure." back="Where are you all heading?" why="The standard warm decline. Next time for sure keeps the door open, and asking where signals genuine interest." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Can I catch up —" say="Can I catch you up later? I need about half an hour here, but I would rather join late than not at all." back="Where will you be?" why="Joining late is a real option people forget. It gets you included without abandoning what you are doing." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Who is going? —" say="Depends. Who is going, and how long are you all planning to stay out?" back="Be honest with me." why="Playful screening. Reads as engaged rather than reluctant, and gets you the information you actually need." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Should we split the bill?" ctx="End of a meal. Small moment, real awkwardness potential." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Sounds good —" say="Sounds good. Down the middle is easiest." back="Have you got cash, or shall we do it by card?" why="Down the middle is the phrase for an even split. Moving straight to the mechanics skips the awkward part." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Let me get this one —" say="Let me get this one. You paid last time and I have been meaning to return the favour." back="You can get the next one." why="Giving a reason makes the offer easy to accept. You can get the next one prevents them feeling indebted." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Only if you let me —" say="Only if you let me get the coffees after. I refuse to be the person who never pays for anything." back="Deal?" why="A counter-offer instead of an argument, and deal is a light one-word close that ends the negotiation cleanly." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< callout type="info" >}}
The single highest-value habit on this page: **propose two specific options**. *Tuesday or Thursday?* moves a plan forward every time, while *let me know* almost always stalls.
{{< /callout >}}
