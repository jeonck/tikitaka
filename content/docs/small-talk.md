title: Small Talk
weight: 2

These are the openers. Nobody asking them wants information — they want to know whether you are available for a conversation. Answer the *invitation*, not the literal question.

{{< tk q="What's new?" ctx="A coworker you see a few times a week." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Not much —" say="Not much, honestly. Same routine, just trying to get through the week." back="You?" why="Not much is the standard answer and nobody minds it. The one-word back keeps the door open without effort." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Actually, something —" say="Actually, something did happen. I finally started going to the gym before work, which I never thought I would survive." back="Have you ever tried the whole early-morning thing?" why="A small change in your routine is safe, specific, and universally relatable. Everyone has an opinion about mornings." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="You go first —" say="You go first. If I answer honestly it is going to be a long story and I have not had enough coffee for it." back="What about you, anything good?" why="Buys you time and signals warmth. Works because it promises a story later instead of refusing one." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Long time no see! How have you been?" ctx="Running into someone you have not seen in months." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Good, good —" say="Good, good. Busy, but the good kind of busy." back="How about you? It has been what, a year?" why="Doubling a word — good, good — is what people actually do when they are pleasantly surprised. It sounds warmer than a single good." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="A lot has changed —" say="A lot has changed, actually. I moved across town and switched teams at work, so I am still finding my feet." back="Are you still at the same place?" why="Two facts, one question. Naming a change invites them to ask which part is going well." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Too long —" say="Way too long. I was just thinking about you last week, which is a terrible excuse for not calling." back="Are you still living around here?" why="Admitting the gap disarms it. Everyone feels slightly guilty about lost contact, so saying it first is a relief for both of you." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Crazy weather, huh?" ctx="An elevator, a bus stop, a queue. The purest small talk there is." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="Tell me about it —" say="Tell me about it. I left the house in a jacket and I have been carrying it around ever since." back="Did you get caught in it?" why="Tell me about it means I agree completely, not please explain. It is one of the most useful three-word replies in English." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="I gave up —" say="I gave up checking the forecast. It has been wrong three days running, so now I just keep an umbrella in my bag and hope." back="Are you the type who checks it every morning?" why="A tiny personal system — the umbrella in the bag — turns a dead topic into a question about habits." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="I have decided —" say="I have decided to take it personally at this point. It only rains on the days I wear the wrong shoes." back="Please tell me it is not just me." why="Please tell me it is not just me is a ready-made bid for agreement. It almost always gets a yes and a story." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="How's your day going so far?" ctx="Mid-morning, someone stops by your desk." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="So far so good —" say="So far so good. Ask me again after lunch." back="How is yours?" why="So far so good is deliberately provisional. The joke about lunch adds warmth without committing you to a real answer." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Slow start —" say="Slow start, but it is picking up. I got through the boring half of my list, so the rest should be easier." back="Are you swamped today?" why="Naming a shape for your day — slow then better — is more interesting than a rating, and swamped is a natural word to offer back." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Honestly? —" say="Honestly? I am still at the stage where I am reading emails without absorbing any of them." back="Are you a get-straight-to-it person, or do you need a warm-up?" why="Honestly? as an opener signals you are about to be real rather than polite, which invites the same in return." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< tk q="Is this seat taken?" ctx="A cafe, a workshop, a shared table." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Return" hint="All yours —" say="All yours, go ahead." back="" why="Two words, complete. Not every exchange needs a pass back — sometimes the ball should stop." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Add" hint="Not at all —" say="Not at all, please. I was just spreading out because nobody was here." back="Are you here for the workshop too?" why="Explaining why your things were spread out prevents any awkwardness, and the follow-up finds common ground fast." >}}
{{< tkline tone="Flip" hint="Only if —" say="Only if you promise not to judge how much stuff I have got on this table." back="Long day for you too?" why="A light condition instead of a plain yes. Low risk, and it converts a seat into a conversation if they want one." >}}
{{< /tk >}}

{{< callout type="info" >}}
Notice that the **Return** route on the last card has no pass back. That is deliberate. Small talk that has finished its job should be allowed to end — forcing a question there would read as pushy.
{{< /callout >}}
